OpenAI doesn't need money. It had $40B after its last round.

So why did it just close a $10B joint venture with 19 private equity firms and guarantee them a 17.5% return?

It bought a sales channel.

Selling AI to enterprises takes 12–18 months. Legal. Procurement. IT review. Board sign-off. PE firms skip all of it — they have 10–20 captive portfolio companies and a single decision-maker with board authority and a direct incentive to create portfolio value.

The Deployment Company isn't a fundraising story. It's OpenAI solving its distribution problem by paying PE firms to be its sales force.

Anthropic did the same thing the same day.

The enterprise AI distribution wars are now being fought in PE boardrooms, not on AWS Marketplace.

## Article Summary

The article argues that OpenAI's $10B "Deployment Company" JV with 19 private equity firms is a distribution strategy, not a fundraising event — PE portfolio structures compress 18-month enterprise sales cycles into weeks. It maps this against a three-channel AI go-to-market framework (direct sales, cloud marketplace, PE portfolio deployment), explains why the 17.5% guaranteed return is a rational risk premium OpenAI is paying to acquire implementation leverage, and closes with practical implications for executives in PE-backed companies and for founders selling AI tooling into the mid-market.
